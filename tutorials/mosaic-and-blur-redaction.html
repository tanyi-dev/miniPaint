How to Pixelate & Blur Sensitive Information Online

A screenshot of a bank statement. A scan of a signed contract. A photo of an ID card with the number covered by a grey smudge. People post images like these every day, and a surprising number of the smudges can be peeled back. Redaction is not "make it look unreadable on my screen" — it is "make it unreadable in the file I hand over". Those are different standards, and only one of them is enforceable.

Why a Blur Is Not a Guarantee

Gaussian blur is a convolution: every pixel is replaced by a weighted average of its neighbours. The fine detail is not encrypted, it is averaged away, and averaging is a lossy operation that research has spent decades learning to invert. Published deblurring work recovers readable text and recognisable faces from blurred images by exploiting what the scene probably looked like — letter shapes, facial structure, the fact that a number has seven digits.

Mosaic is worse for small type. Block averaging removes pixel detail but preserves the silhouette of the characters: a phone number stays a row of dark blobs in the same rhythm as the digits. At a small block size the rhythm is enough to read a date, a postcode, or a four-digit PIN.

There is one more leak that has nothing to do with the pixels: the layer stack. If you blur with a layer effect and later move or delete that effect, the content comes back. The exported file is safe; the project file is not. When the image is legally sensitive, work on a copy and export it, and treat the project file as confidential too.

Match the Method to the Risk

There is no single correct tool. What you are hiding decides how much destruction is acceptable:

What you are hiding Use this Why
Password, PIN, ID or card numberSolid filled rectangleNothing left to reconstruct. Short strings are guessable even from heavy blur.
SignatureSolid rectangle over the whole strokeBlur keeps the slant and rhythm a forgery works from.
Full name, address on an envelopeSolid rectangle or mosaic at Size 25+Letters are large; a coarse mosaic is enough.
Bystander's faceMosaic, Size 15–25Blocking the features reads as intentional and is not reversible in practice.
Chat screenshot, one messageBlur brush or mosaicLow risk, high volume. Speed matters more than perfect coverage.

One Rule Before Any Method

The Mosaic and Gaussian Blur effects both process the entire layer, not the selection. Confirming the dialog pixels the whole screenshot, which is rarely what you want. Two ways around it, both of which you can do in the browser:

  • Duplicate, then erase back. Layer → Duplicate, apply the effect to the copy, then erase the areas that should stay sharp with the Eraser tool. The untouched original sits underneath and shows through.
  • Draw the cover on top. Add a new layer and draw filled rectangles with the Shape tool over the sensitive areas. Nothing underneath is modified, and a filled shape is the strongest redaction available.
Method 1: Mosaic (pixelate)

Duplicate the layer first. Select the copy and choose Effects → Mosaic. The dialog has a single Size control from 1 to 100, expressed as a percentage of the layer's shorter side: 10 on a 1000 px screenshot means roughly 100 px blocks, which is coarse. Confirm, then erase the parts of the copy that should stay readable. Text needs a coarser setting than faces — check at 100% zoom, not in the fitted preview, where every setting looks destructive.

Method 2: Gaussian blur

Also on a duplicated layer: Effects → Common Filters → Gaussian Blur. The single Percentage slider runs 0–50. Because this is a live layer filter, the blur stays adjustable after you apply it — unlike Mosaic, which rewrites the pixels. That flexibility is convenient while you work and risky if the project file ever leaves your machine, so export a flattened copy for anything you publish.

Method 3: The blur brush

Pick the Blur tool in the toolbar and paint straight over the text. The brush defaults to size 30 with strength 1; raise the size to cover a full line in two or three passes and repeat the stroke rather than pressing harder. This is the fastest option for chat screenshots and the least precise — the brush edge leaves a soft rim where a pixel of the original can survive, so do not use it for ID numbers.

Method 4: Solid block (the safe default)

Add a layer, choose the Shape tool with a rectangle, and fill it with a colour close to the page background. Draw it from a few pixels above the first character to a few pixels below the last, and wider than the text on both sides. A solid block carries no information at all, survives screenshots and re-compression, and is the only method that stays safe under a legal review.

Cover More Than the Text

Most failed redactions are not a weak blur; they are a tight box. Four things sit outside the glyphs and each one can leak:

  • Antialiased edges. Text is rendered with soft edges, and those edge pixels fall outside the letter but inside the reading. Extend the cover three to five pixels past every side.
  • Shadows and show-through. A scan of a two-sided page often reveals the reverse side faintly; a photo of a document on a desk picks up the shadow of your hand. Cover those areas too, or they describe what is underneath.
  • Surrounding context. Hiding an account number but leaving the bank's name, the branch, and the statement period visible can be enough to identify the person.
  • Selection handles and cursors. Screenshot captured while a tool was active? The handles tell a reader which field you were about to edit.

Check the Export, Not the Canvas

Before publishing, close the loop:

  • Export, then reopen the exported file. A layer you forgot to hide is invisible in a flattened JPEG and visible in the project. The export is the artefact that matters.
  • Zoom to 400%. Soft edges, residual strokes, and half-covered characters show up there and nowhere else.
  • Try to read it yourself. Turn your monitor brightness up. If you can make out two of four digits from the shape, that is the weak spot.
  • Look at the thumbnail. Chat apps shrink images before sending. A redaction that works at full size sometimes thins out at 20%, though the reverse — appearing safe small and readable large — is the more common trap.
💡 Rule of thumb: the more valuable the string, the less you blur it. Passwords, card numbers and signatures get a solid block; faces and paragraphs can take a mosaic.

Quick Answers

Is a blurred area really reversible?

A blur is not reversible in the strict sense, because the detail is gone. But for content with strong structure, such as printed text, faces or a signature stroke, published deblurring research shows a useful amount can be inferred back. Mosaic is weaker still on small text: the block grid keeps the shape of the letters. The test is whether a person can guess the characters from context. If they can, so can software. Passwords, ID numbers and signatures should get a solid block, not a blur.

Should I redact the whole page or just the sensitive line?

Only what needs hiding, but cover each area generously: the full height of the characters plus three to five pixels beyond every edge. Text sits close to the box you draw, and an antialiased edge or a shadow can survive a tight crop. Redacting half the page is not safer either. It signals that something specific was in there and invites a closer look at whatever is left.

What is the safest way to hide a signature?

A solid filled rectangle over the whole stroke area, from the first letter to the last, including the pen shadow. Mosaic and blur leave the slant and rhythm of a signature readable, which is exactly what a forgery needs. Draw the rectangle with the Shape tool, fill it with a dark colour sampled from the page background, and check that no ink shows at the corners.
